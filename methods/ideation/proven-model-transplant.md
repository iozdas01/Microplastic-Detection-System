## Core Insight

Take a company whose model is already proven in one context and transplant it to a context where it doesn't yet exist — a new geography ("DoorDash for Latin America") or a new vertical ("Amazon Go for all retail"). The mechanism that makes this attractive: the underlying model is *de-risked*. Someone has already proven the unit economics, the demand, and the operational playbook. Your remaining bet is narrower — does the same model work in the new context?

But this recipe carries the highest SISP risk of all the idea-generation methods. "Airbnb for Y" ideas frequently have no real problem in the target context — the founder copies the *solution* and assumes the problem transfers. Friedman's guidance: treat these ideas as guilty until proven innocent. The value is in de-risking the model; the danger is skipping verification that the problem actually exists where you're transplanting it.

## Process

1. Identify a company/model that is clearly succeeding in its original context.
2. List the *enabling conditions* that made it work there: market maturity, infrastructure, regulation, consumer behavior, willingness to pay.
3. Choose a target context (geography or vertical) where the model doesn't yet exist.
4. Check each enabling condition: does it hold in the target context, or was the original success dependent on something absent here?
5. **Verify the problem independently** in the new context. Apply the SISP guard
   documented in [`../intake/sisp-detection.md`](../intake/sisp-detection.md):
   do people there actually have the pain, or are you just relocating a
   solution? During a shotgun, use the analogy to pressure-test the active
   hunch. Record a different transplant as `out_of_scope_alternative` until
   synthesis finds solution-independent problem evidence.
6. Assess your founder/market fit for the new context — do you understand this geography or vertical well enough to execute the local differences?

## Example

**Rappi**: DoorDash's on-demand delivery model transplanted to Latin America, where the enabling conditions (dense cities, smartphone penetration, low labor cost) held and local logistics were underserved. **Standard Cognition**: took Amazon Go's cashierless-checkout technology and transplanted the model to serve *all* retailers rather than one company's own stores.

## Limitations

- Highest SISP risk of any recipe — the problem often does not transfer with the solution. Always pair with SISP Detection.
- "It doesn't exist here yet" may mean it *can't* work here (a missing enabling condition), not that it's an open opportunity.
- The original company may expand into your target context and out-execute you with a head start.
- Copying a model rarely comes with strong founder/market fit — you may know the model but not the new context.

## Connection to the Loop

During a shotgun, test whether a proven model actually supports the active
hunch's segment and problem. Do not replace the hunch with the analogy. Outside
the shotgun, the method can still generate candidates, but each candidate needs
an independent-problem SISP test before assumption extraction.
