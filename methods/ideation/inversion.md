## Core Insight

Conventional thinking locks solutions into the assumed direction of causality. If "more people going somewhere = more emissions" feels like a law, nobody builds Uber Pool. Inversion asks: what if the assumed outcome ran the other way? This forces you to find mechanisms that produce the opposite result — which almost always reveals non-obvious solutions and often reveals the assumptions that are actually constraints vs. the ones that are negotiable.

Applied to thesis mutation: when evidence contradicts your thesis, inversion is a structured way to reframe rather than abandon. If "automation can replace the human operator" is proving false, flip it: what if automation made the operator MORE productive rather than replacing them? That's a different product, different buyer, different market — and maybe a truer one.

## Process

1. Identify the "obvious" outcome of a problem or situation
2. State it as a directional claim: "More X = more Y"
3. Ask: "What if more X = less Y?" or "What if the opposite were true?"
4. Take the inversion seriously — don't dismiss it immediately
5. Find the mechanism that would make the inverted claim true
6. Evaluate whether that mechanism exists or could be created

## Examples

**Original:** More people in a small space going somewhere = more emissions
**Inverted:** More people going somewhere together = fewer emissions per person
**Mechanism:** Shared mobility, dense neighborhoods, carpooling → Uber Pool, bike share, transit

**Original:** More trees burning = more emissions
**Inverted:** More trees burning = less atmospheric carbon
**Mechanism:** Pyrolysis in low-oxygen environments → biochar (sequesters carbon)

**For thesis mutation:**
**Original thesis:** Automation replaces the human operator
**Evidence against:** Buyers don't trust full autonomy for business-critical work
**Inverted:** What if the automation makes operators safer and faster, not unnecessary?
**New thesis:** Assistive tooling for human operators → different wedge, different buyer trust requirement

## Limitations

- Not all inversions are physically or economically possible
- Requires domain knowledge to evaluate whether the inversion has a real mechanism
- Can generate interesting ideas that have no viable path (clever but unbuildable)
- Works better for reframing business/market assumptions than for hard technical constraints
- The inversion is a hypothesis — it still needs the same assumption-testing rigor as the original

## Connection to the Loop

Use at two points:
- **Shotgun ideation:** invert one assumption inside the active hunch and report
  what that does to the same hunch; a materially different opportunity remains
  an out-of-scope alternative until synthesis and founder confirmation
- **Thesis mutation:** when evidence contradicts a core assumption, invert it before abandoning the thesis — the inverted version may be the real opportunity
