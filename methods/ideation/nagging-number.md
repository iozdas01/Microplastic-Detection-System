## Core Insight

A single striking statistic can reveal a structural inefficiency the market hasn't priced in. The mechanism: markets settle into equilibria that hide their own dysfunction, and a number that is absurd on its face ("73% of trucks drive back empty," "87% of new insurance agents quit within 3 years") is a crack in that equilibrium — it points to trapped value or an approaching structural break. Hunting deliberately for such numbers is a repeatable ideation trigger, because the emotional reaction ("that's crazy") is a reliable detector of an anomaly worth investigating.

## Process

1. Pick domains where you have some edge (past jobs, consulting exposure — Wand read for 3 months across candidate spaces).
2. Read for numbers, not narratives. Hunt for ratios, percentages, and trends that seem absurd.
3. When a number makes you say "that can't be right," don't dismiss it — write it down. That reaction is the signal.
4. Ask what structural fact the number implies: waste (empty trucks), a demographic time-bomb (aging agents leaving faster than they're replaced), or mispriced risk.
5. Ask whether the structure is about to break, and what company profits from the break.
6. Cross-check excitement: does the number nag you at night / in the shower? If not, it's interesting but not yours (Wand dropped trucking despite the 73% figure — it didn't excite him).

## Example

Assaf Wand found the average insurance agent was 58 (rising to 61), fewer than half as many agents worked the field as a decade earlier, and 87% of new agents quit within 3 years. "It's like social security — not enough people coming in, a lot leaving. Therefore there needs to be a change." That nagging cluster of numbers → Hippo, "a Geico for home insurance."

## Limitations

- A striking number proves an anomaly exists, not that it's addressable or that you can profit from it (several companies chased the empty-trucks number; it's a hard problem).
- Statistics can be stale, cherry-picked, or misdefined — verify the source and the exact definition.
- The number tells you WHERE to look, not WHAT to build; pair it with a real pain and a why-now.

## Connection to the Loop

During a shotgun, verify whether the number supports or contradicts the active
hunch's market or timing claim. A number pointing elsewhere is an out-of-scope
alternative, not permission to switch subjects. Pair with Why-Now and
Predecessor Analysis.
