## Core Insight

Segmentation and incumbent avoidance answer one decision: where should the
first hunch enter? A 2×2 can reveal a structurally attractive group, while
incumbent posture explains whether that group is genuinely underserved or
ignored for a good reason. Treating disdain as a separate lens can produce a
celebrated niche with no economics; treating segmentation without incumbent
behavior can produce a neat quadrant already controlled by strong suppliers.

## Process

1. Build a segment universe from the evidence packet. Define segments as
   company/customer type + affected role + workflow circumstance.
2. Compare segments on evidence-backed dimensions: recurrence, consequence,
   workaround effort, existing spend or headcount, reachability, switching
   cost, growth, and substitute quality.
3. Use a 2×2 only when two dimensions materially determine attractiveness for
   the emerging hunch. Name why those axes matter. Do not draw arbitrary axes
   merely to complete the method.
4. Map incumbent posture for each segment using actions and attributable
   language:
   - actively served;
   - served poorly because the business model conflicts;
   - ignored as too small, low-status, fragmented, or uneconomic;
   - inaccessible to incumbents for technical or regulatory reasons;
   - unknown.
5. Distinguish dismissal from rational avoidance. Test whether the segment has
   enough pain, spend, aggregation, and path outward to support a company.
6. Select the smallest segment where pain is sharp, evidence is independent,
   current substitutes fail, access is plausible, and incumbent response is
   constrained.
7. Record adjacent segments that could become a path out. A narrow wedge
   without credible adjacency is a niche, not necessarily a venture market.

## Example

A high-frequency, low-provider-loyalty healthcare segment may be structurally
attractive to a marketplace. If incumbents also avoid it because transactions
are fragmented and low-value, that is not automatically a moat. The wedge is
credible only when evidence shows sufficient aggregated demand, reachable
buyers, and a path into adjacent care categories.

## Limitations

- Segments and axes can be chosen to justify a preferred answer.
- Public evidence may underrepresent small or quiet segments.
- Incumbent silence is not disdain; require attributable statements or
  consistent behavior.
- Incumbents may enter once the segment becomes attractive.
- This method selects a candidate wedge; it does not validate market size,
  acquisition cost, or willingness to pay.

## Connection to the Loop

Run after pain clusters and the JTBD/substitute map exist. During a shotgun,
test whether the active hunch's segment is the credible first wedge. Record a
better segment as an out-of-scope alternative; do not silently switch the run
subject.
