## Core Insight

When you have deep expertise in one field and begin learning another, you see problems that insiders of the second field are blind to — because they take the status quo for granted. The mechanism: every field has accumulated workarounds, accepted limitations, and "that's just how it works" assumptions. An outsider with a different toolkit pattern-matches differently and immediately asks "why isn't this solved by X?" where X is something obvious in their home field.

This is distinct from Cross-Domain Persona (imagining how someone else would approach a problem). This is about deliberately developing your own knowledge combination so that gaps become visible to you organically. PG: "The clash of domains is a particularly fruitful source of ideas."

The preparation part matters: the idea comes from an external stimulus hitting a prepared mind. You can't control the stimulus, but you can build the preparation — and domain collisions are how you build it.

## Process

1. Map your existing deep expertise: where do you genuinely know more than most? (domain, technology, industry, workflow)
2. Identify an adjacent or completely different field you could meaningfully learn — not skim, but get deep enough to understand how practitioners actually work
3. As you learn the new field, specifically notice: what do practitioners accept as just-how-it-works that your home-field expertise says could be solved?
4. Pay attention to the moments of "wait, why doesn't anyone just...?" — those are the collision moments
5. Don't immediately try to build a company. Just note the gaps. The pattern emerges over time.

## Example

PG + Viaweb: deep programming expertise colliding with e-commerce. Online stores were all hand-built HTML pages. "It was obvious to us as programmers that these sites would have to be generated by software." Ecommerce insiders accepted hand-built pages as normal. Programmer outsiders saw it as obviously solvable.

The founders of most developer tools: deep programming expertise, start using a domain (payments, HR, infra) and see that the tools are built for non-programmers in ways that make no sense.

## Note on relationship to Cross-Domain Persona

Cross-Domain Persona (see `unused/cross-domain-persona.md`) is a thought experiment: imagining how someone else would approach a problem. This method is about building YOUR actual expertise collision so that gaps become visible to you naturally, not through simulation. The outputs are different: Cross-Domain Persona generates ideas quickly in a session; Domain Expertise Collision builds the conditions for organic idea generation over months.

## Limitations

- Requires real investment in learning the new domain — skimming doesn't produce the collision
- Works best as a long-term founder positioning strategy, not a technique to run in a session
- The collision only produces ideas if your home-field expertise is actually applicable to the new domain
- Can produce technically solvable ideas that have no commercial demand — still needs the full assumption loop
