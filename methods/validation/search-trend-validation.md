## Core Insight

Search volume is one of the cheapest, most honest behavioral proxies for demand — people search for what they actually want, privately, with no incentive to please you. Comparing your term's volume and *growth trajectory* against well-understood benchmark terms calibrates both magnitude ("is this big?") and momentum ("is this growing?") in an afternoon, for free. The comparison is what makes it work: absolute search numbers are meaningless without a familiar yardstick.

## Process

1. Turn your hypothesized demand into the terms real people would search ("urgent care").
2. Pull the trend (Google Trends or a keyword tool): current volume and the trajectory over years.
3. Choose benchmark terms whose size you intuitively understand, spanning your expected range (Fernandez compared vs. "primary care," "Viagra," "birth control").
4. Compare magnitude and, more importantly, slope — a smaller term growing fast can beat a bigger flat one.
5. Treat "huge and growing by comparison" as a real behavioral signal; treat flat/declining as a warning even if the market is nominally large.

## Example

To validate the "convenient care" quadrant, Heather Fernandez checked Google Trends for "urgent care" against "primary care," "Viagra," and "birth control." Urgent care was "huge and growing by comparison" — "Holy shit, this is really big." That behavioral signal, plus her 2×2, made SolvHealth's opportunity and go-to-market clear.

## Limitations

- Search reflects awareness and interest, not willingness to pay or a business model — high search ≠ monetizable.
- New categories people can't yet name won't show up (you can't search for what you can't articulate — see Stated Want vs. Latent Need).
- Trends can be driven by news spikes, seasonality, or ambiguous multi-meaning terms; read the curve, not a single point.

## Connection to the Loop

A cheap, fast validation for the market (and timing) assumption — often the first quantitative check to run after a market hypothesis, before spending on customer research or building. Pairs with Segment and Wedge Map (locate the entry segment → confirm its behavior is growing) and the Nagging Number (a shocking stat → does behavior corroborate it?).
