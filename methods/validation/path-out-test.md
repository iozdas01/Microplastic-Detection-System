## Core Insight

The Well Test establishes necessary condition: narrow, deep demand. But it's not sufficient. A startup that can only ever serve its initial niche is a niche product, not a company. The Path-Out Test asks the sufficient-condition question: is the initial beachhead a natural launchpad to a bigger market, or is it a dead end?

PG: "Facebook was a good idea because it started with a small market there was a fast path out of." Harvard students → all colleges → everyone. The path was fast because colleges are structurally similar — if it works at one, it works at all. Microsoft: Altair Basic → other machines → other languages → operating systems → applications → IPO. Each step was a natural adjacency.

The key test for the beachhead: are the people in it doing something that *lots more people will be doing in the future*? If yes, the beachhead size matters less than its trajectory. Early smartphone users were a tiny beachhead — but they were doing something nearly everyone would eventually do.

## Process

1. Name the initial beachhead precisely — the specific group who urgently wants this (output of the Well Test)
2. Ask: what are the natural adjacencies from this group? Who is structurally similar enough that if it works here, it works there?
3. Trace a plausible expansion path: beachhead → step 2 → step 3. Name each step.
4. Check the trajectory: is the beachhead doing something lots more people will be doing in the future? Or is it a stable niche with no growth pressure?
5. Ask the hardest version: if this could *only* ever serve the initial group, is that still a business? (Sometimes yes — but be honest about it)

## Examples

**Facebook:** Harvard (thousands of students) → all colleges → everyone. Fast path because the product worked identically at every college. Colleges are the same product, different instance.

**Airbnb:** Air mattresses at conventions → spare rooms → entire homes → hotels. Each step was a natural relaxation of one constraint (type of accommodation) while the core job-to-be-done (cheap, local lodging) remained constant.

**Stripe:** Developer-first payments → any developer → any business accepting payments online. The beachhead (developers who built web apps) was doing something that all businesses would eventually do.

## Limitations

- Paths out are often not predictable in advance — Airbnb's founders didn't foresee the expansion, it "forced itself upon them gradually." Don't require a perfect map.
- Some valuable companies have no path out and are deliberately niche — the Path-Out Test is for people building toward large companies, not niche tools
- The path can seem obvious and still be blocked (regulatory, network effects from incumbents, distribution locked up)

## Connection to the Loop

Pair this with the Well Test: Well Test = necessary condition (is demand narrow and deep?), Path-Out Test = sufficient condition (is there a natural expansion from the beachhead?). Run both. A good startup idea passes both. A niche product passes only the first.
