## Core Insight

Not all problems are equal — and the difference between a fundable idea and a lifestyle business often comes down to where the pain sits on two axes: how bad it is (magnitude) and how often it occurs (frequency). A pain that is both severe and recurring drives urgent, price-insensitive demand. A pain that is only one of the two generates interest but rarely urgency. A pain that is neither produces no behavior change at all.

The mechanism: buyers budget around frequency × severity. High-frequency, high-magnitude pain generates recurring revenue because the problem never goes away. High-magnitude, low-frequency pain generates large deal sizes because each occurrence is expensive. Low-magnitude, high-frequency pain produces engagement but not willingness to pay — people live with it because each incident is cheap enough to ignore. Low-magnitude, low-frequency pain is invisible to the market.

This classification does not tell you whether a market exists — it tells you what *kind* of market exists and what business model and pricing logic follows from it.

## Process

1. Name the specific pain in the buyer's language, not yours
2. Assess frequency: how often does this buyer face this problem? Daily, weekly, quarterly, once in a career?
3. Assess magnitude: when it occurs, what does it cost in money, time, risk, or reputation?
4. Plot on the 2×2:
   - High magnitude + high frequency → **Unicorn Zone**: recurring, urgent, price-insensitive (Uber, Slack)
   - High magnitude + low frequency → **Enterprise Zone**: large deal sizes, long sales cycles, heavy ROI justification (Palantir, Workday)
   - Low magnitude + high frequency → **Vitamin Zone**: engagement without monetization; hard to convert
   - Low magnitude + low frequency → **Dead Zone**: do not proceed
5. Adjust your business model and sales motion to match the zone — not the zone to match your preferred model
6. If you can't place the pain confidently, the pain assumption is not yet precise enough to test

## Example

Uber sits in the Unicorn Zone: the pain of not getting a reliable ride is both high-magnitude (you miss the meeting, the flight) and high-frequency (every trip in a new city). That produced explosive, price-insensitive early adoption. Contrast with Quibi: low-magnitude entertainment gap (you can already watch Netflix on your phone), low-frequency occurrence. Dead Zone. $1.75B raised, dead in six months.

## Limitations

- Magnitude and frequency are buyer-dependent — classify for your specific buyer archetype, not the general population
- This is a filter, not a validator. Passing it tells you what type of market you're in, not whether your solution fits or whether you can win
- Self-reported magnitude is unreliable — use behavioral evidence (what have they actually paid to solve it?) to calibrate
- Low frequency ≠ no market. Enterprise Zone companies have built billions on low-frequency, high-magnitude pain — but sales motion and capital requirements are fundamentally different

## Connection to the Loop

Use as the first filter on the **pain assumption** before designing any interview, experiment, or landing page. If you cannot place the pain in the Unicorn or Enterprise Zone with behavioral evidence, the pain assumption is unconfirmed and no downstream validation is meaningful.
