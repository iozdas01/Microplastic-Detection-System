## Core Insight

Founders evaluate ideas with a single gut feeling, which lets one attractive dimension (a huge market, or a personal obsession) drown out fatal weaknesses on other dimensions. The fix is to decompose idea quality into four orthogonal criteria, score each 1–10, and take the average. Averaging is the mechanism: it forces you to confront the criteria where you score low instead of anchoring on the one where you score high.

The four criteria (from Dalton Caldwell) are each checkable against *external evidence*, not just opinion:

- **Market size** — checkable by finding existing large companies doing something similar. If big companies already make money adjacent to your idea, the market is real.
- **Founder/market fit** — checkable against your track record. Are you genuinely an expert here? (Flexport's founder had 10 years in international trade.)
- **Pain certainty** — checkable against your own lived experience. Do you personally know the problem is painful?
- **Novel insight** — checkable by asking what you know that others have missed. (Airbnb's founders had actually hosted strangers, so they knew it could work.)

The point isn't the precise number — it's that scoring each dimension separately surfaces the weak one you were about to ignore.

## Process

1. Write the idea down in one sentence.
2. Score 1–10 on **market size**: search for existing large companies doing something similar. No large adjacent companies = low score.
3. Score 1–10 on **founder/market fit**: are you (or a cofounder) a real expert in this domain? Be honest about depth.
4. Score 1–10 on **pain certainty**: do you have direct personal experience of this problem, or are you guessing?
5. Score 1–10 on **novel insight**: what do you know that others have missed? A generic idea with no insight scores low here.
6. Take the average. Compare the averages across your candidate ideas to triage where to spend the next few weeks.
7. Look hardest at any single low score — that's the assumption to test first.

## Example

**Flexport**: founder/market fit ~10 (10 years in international trade). **Airbnb**: novel insight ~10 — the founders had actually tried hosting strangers and knew it worked, an insight most people dismissed as crazy. In both cases a single very high dimension carried an idea that looked weak on a naive gut check.

## Limitations

- Scores are subjective estimates; two founders will score the same idea differently.
- The average can hide a fatal zero — a 1 on market size averaged with three 9s still yields a "good" score but a dead company. Treat any single very low score as a veto to investigate, not a number to average away.
- Early-stage scores are guesses; the point is triage between ideas, not a verdict on any one.
- A high score does not validate demand — it prioritizes which idea to run through the assumption-testing loop, nothing more.

## Connection to the Loop

Use immediately after **ideation**, when you have several candidate ideas and need to decide which one earns the next few weeks of assumption testing. The lowest-scoring criterion tells you which assumption (market, pain, or founder-market-fit) to extract and test first.
