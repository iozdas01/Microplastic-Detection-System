## Core Insight

Every validation method asks the founder to push — design an interview, build a landing page, send cold outreach, run ads. The Pull Metric inverts the diagnostic: instead of measuring how the market responds to your push, it measures whether the market is pulling toward you without prompting. Market pull — buyers emailing you to ask when they can buy, peers spontaneously introducing you to their colleagues, users returning to a beta product they didn't need to return to — is the rarest and most reliable signal in early-stage validation. It cannot be manufactured and it cannot be faked.

The mechanism: pull behavior reveals that the buyer's pain is active right now, not hypothetical. A buyer who emails you unprompted has a problem they are actively trying to solve today. A buyer who introduces you to three peers without being asked has a pain they believe their peers share. These behaviors are costly to produce — they require the buyer to spend social capital, time, or reputation. Any costly signal is more informative than a cheap one.

The absence of pull does not mean the idea is bad. Most early ideas produce no pull because the founder hasn't reached the right buyers yet. The presence of pull, however, is an unconditional green light: stop analyzing and start building.

## Process

1. During outreach, interviews, and demos, watch for unprompted behavior: Does the buyer ask "when can I buy this?" before you've mentioned pricing? Do they forward your email to a colleague without being asked? Do they bring up your product in their next meeting with a peer and then connect you?
2. After a landing page or pre-sale goes live: are inbound emails arriving from people who found you without a direct push? Is anyone sharing the page organically?
3. After beta access: do users return without a re-engagement email? Do they ask for features rather than complaining about missing ones?
4. Log every pull signal — date, who, what behavior, what they said — in the Evidence Ledger. Pull signals are rare enough that each one is a named data point.
5. Calibrate against your segment: in B2B, one pull signal from a VP of Operations at a target company is stronger evidence than 50 email signups from anonymous web traffic. Weight by buyer quality, not by count.
6. If you are deep into validation with zero pull signals, ask why: wrong buyer segment, wrong problem framing, or right idea at the wrong time. The absence of pull is a diagnostic, not a verdict.

## Example

The article's framing: "Are customers asking *you* when they can buy? Are they emailing you to follow up?" — contrasted with "If you are constantly pushing and begging, you don't have it." The Slack origin story is the canonical pull example: Slack was an internal tool at Tiny Speck, and other companies kept asking to use it after seeing it in action. That inbound demand — before any marketing — was the signal that made Stewart Butterfield pivot the whole company toward it.

## Limitations

- Pull is rare in the very earliest stages — the first 30 days of outreach almost never produce pull because the founder hasn't reached enough people. Absence of pull in week 1 is not signal.
- Pull from within your network (friends, ex-colleagues, investors) is discounted. Pull from cold contacts — people who found you without a warm intro — is the meaningful variant.
- Pull can be manufactured through artificial scarcity ("only 10 spots"). Watch for this in your own experiments — if you created scarcity to trigger pull, the signal is about the scarcity, not the pain.
- In highly regulated or conservative industries, pull may manifest as "would you come talk to my team?" rather than "when can I buy?" — recognize the industry-specific form.

## Connection to the Loop

Monitor throughout the entire validation phase as a background signal — it doesn't require a dedicated experiment. A single pull signal should immediately surface as a priority data point in the Evidence Ledger and may change the direction of the next validation step (e.g., accelerating the LOI request with the pulling buyer). No pull after 30+ contacts is a flag that the buyer segment or pain framing needs revision. Pairs with Nurture Coefficient (measures push difficulty) to triangulate overall market receptivity.
