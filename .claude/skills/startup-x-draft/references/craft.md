# Craft rules for X posts

What actually makes a post land, and what makes it read as growth-hacking. The second list
matters more than the first: this account belongs to a technical founder whose readers
include the plant managers, integrators, and lab researchers they are also cold-messaging.
A post that reads as engagement bait costs a reply rate, not just a like.

---

## The three-beat structure

Every post is attention → value → (sometimes) action. Each beat has a price the reader pays:
reading costs attention, replying costs time, clicking costs both plus intent. The post has
to be worth more than the beat costs, or the reader scrolls.

Most posts on this account should stop after **value**. A post with no CTA that teaches
something real is a stronger follow trigger than a post that asks for the follow. Ask for
something only when the ask is itself useful to the reader ("if you run a plant with X, I
want to hear how you handle Y" is a real invitation; "follow for more" is a toll booth).

---

## Hooks

The hook sets intent, not just attention — it tells the reader what kind of value is coming
so they know whether to keep reading. Three patterns that work for this account:

| Pattern | Shape | Example shape for this account |
|---|---|---|
| **Counter-consensus** | The thing everyone believes, then the contradiction | "Everyone building humanoids is buying environment data. The labs I talked to don't want it." |
| **Specific number** | A real figure from real work, then what it cost to learn | "65 logged interviews in. The single most repeated sentence wasn't about cost." |
| **Named frustration** | The pain, stated in the reader's own words | "Your robot works in the lab and dies in the plant. It's not the robot." |

Mechanics that hold regardless of pattern:

- **Line one under ~80 characters.** Longer truncates on mobile and the claim dies mid-word.
- **Strongest claim first**, not the setup. The context can go on line three.
- **Numbers and nouns, never adjectives.** "4 of 7 labs" beats "most labs". "Cut commissioning
  from 6 weeks to 9 days" beats "dramatically faster".
- **Line two opens a gap** the rest of the post closes. One short line, not a paragraph.
- **Audience qualifier when the claim is narrow** — "if you sell into brownfield plants",
  "for anyone training manipulation policies". It costs three words and filters for the
  readers whose reply is worth having.

Dead openers, no exceptions: "Some thoughts on…", "Here's a thread about…", "Let's talk
about…", "Unpopular opinion:". They announce that nothing has happened yet.

## Value and proof

Deliver something usable inside a minute. The strongest asset this account has is **primary
evidence nobody else holds** — findings from real interviews and real site work. That is
rarer on X than any opinion, so it should carry most posts.

Proof in descending order of strength:

1. **A number from work actually done** — interview counts, reply rates, a measured delta.
2. **A verbatim thing someone said**, anonymized to a role and company type.
3. **A named public source** — a paper, a job posting, a procurement notice.
4. **Transferable experience** — "I spent N years doing X" as context, not as argument.

Weak proof is worse than none: a claim with a made-up-sounding number invites a reply
asking for the source, and there won't be one. See `disclosure.md` for what may be said
about whom.

## CTAs

At most one, and usually zero. When there is one, it should be the kind of ask that gives
the reader something:

- An open question the reader has a real answer to.
- An offer to share something concrete, delivered in the replies, not gated.
- A link to something that stands on its own.

**Off-limits on this account** — these are creator-economy tactics that convert on general
X and read as cheap to a technical audience:

- "Comment 'X' and I'll DM it" — reply-farming, obvious to anyone who has seen it once.
- "Link in bio" funnels.
- "Follow for more" and any variant that asks without giving.
- Fake-milestone flexing, engagement-pod language, "🧵👇" as a whole line.
- Multiple competing CTAs — they split attention and none of them get taken.

---

## Conversion killers

Cut these on sight:

- **Vague benefit with no number.** "Big improvement" is noise.
- **A hook the body doesn't pay off.** Overpromising costs the next post's attention too.
- **Hedging stacked on hedging.** "It seems like it might possibly be the case that" — say it
  or cut it. If it needs a hedge, one is enough, and it goes at the end.
- **LinkedIn voice.** "Excited to share", "humbled", "thrilled to announce", "game-changer".
- **Explaining the joke.** If the post has a punchline, it ends there.
- **The em-dash-heavy, tricolon-heavy AI cadence.** "It's not just X — it's Y." "The best
  part? Z." If a line pattern-matches to generated text, rewrite it flatter and shorter.

## Replies

Replying under someone else's post is the cheapest distribution this account has: the
author's audience sees it, and a good reply earns profile clicks from exactly the readers
the posts are for. The craft differs from posts, mostly by subtraction:

- **Add something, or don't reply.** The only replies worth sending carry a number, a
  firsthand experience, or a disagreement with a receipt. "Great post" and restating the
  post in different words are invisible.
- **One beat, two or three lines.** A reply is value only — no hook ladder, no CTA, ever.
- **Disagree with the claim, never the person.** The author decides whether the thread
  continues; a reply that reads as a dunk ends it.
- **Don't hijack.** A reply that pivots to this account's thesis unprompted reads as an ad.
  The thesis earns a mention only when it is the direct answer to what the post asked.
- **Same disclosure ladder as posts.** A reply is just as public and gets quote-posted
  just as easily.
- **Check the author against the active idea's `contacts.md` first.** Replying publicly to
  someone mid-outreach is an outreach move — it can warm a cold thread or step on a drafted
  message. If the author is a contact, surface that and decide with the founder before
  drafting anything.

---

## Formatting

- One idea per line. Line breaks are the only formatting X gives; use them.
- Threads only when the content genuinely has sequential parts. A thread built from one idea
  padded to seven tweets is visible as padding.
- No hashtags. They do nothing on X now and mark the account as migrated from elsewhere.
- Emoji only if the voice profile says the founder uses them. Default: none.
- Under 280 for single posts. If it needs 400 characters, it's a thread or it's two posts.

---

## Iteration

Track hook → profile-click rate and post → reply rate, not likes. Replies from the actual
target segment are the metric that matters here, because they double as warm outreach: a
plant engineer arguing with a post is a better lead than a cold DM.

Change one thing per test. Lines one and two first — they carry most of the variance.
