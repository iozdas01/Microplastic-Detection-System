# Disclosure ladder — what may be said about whom

The repo holds private material: named contacts, verbatim DM quotes, interview notes from
people who agreed to a founder conversation and not to being quoted in public. A post that
burns one of those relationships costs far more than the post earns, and it is not
retractable — screenshots outlive deletions.

So every factual claim in a draft gets placed on this ladder before it ships. When a claim
sits above the line the founder has cleared, either anonymize it down a tier or cut it.

---

## Tier 0 — always safe

- The founder's own reasoning, hypotheses, and changes of mind.
- Method and process: how assumptions are ranked, what a kill condition is, why a hunch was
  retired.
- Anything already public: papers, job postings, procurement notices, company marketing
  copy, conference talks, public product pages.
- Aggregate counts of the founder's own activity — "65 interviews logged", "18 invites sent,
  4 replies".

## Tier 1 — safe when anonymized

Findings from private conversations, stripped to a role and a company *category*:

- ✅ "A team training humanoid manipulation policies told me the environment data isn't the
  bottleneck — it's joint-level dynamics."
- ❌ "CosmicBrain's research lead told me…"

Rules for anonymizing well:
- Role + company type + rough size. Never the name, never the exact funding round, never a
  detail that identifies them to anyone in their industry ("the only European lab doing X").
- If the category is so narrow that one company obviously fits, widen it or drop the claim.
- Paraphrase rather than quote. A verbatim sentence is searchable and traceable back to a
  DM thread.

## Tier 2 — needs explicit founder permission, asked for per post

- Naming a company or a person.
- Quoting anyone verbatim, even anonymously.
- Any characterization of a named company's internal situation, roadmap, or spending.
- Numbers a contact shared about their own business — budgets, headcount, failure rates.

Ask directly and get a yes before drafting, not after: "This post names <company>. Cleared?"

## Tier 3 — never

- Anything that identifies a private individual who did not consent to being public.
- Screenshots of DMs, emails, or LinkedIn threads.
- Competitive intelligence about a named company that was shared in confidence.
- Anything that would tell a contact's employer something the contact told the founder.
- Contact details, links to individual profiles as targets, or anything resembling a
  target list.

---

## Evidence-grade honesty

`evidence.md` grades every entry, and posts have to respect the grade. Stating an untested
assumption as a finding is the fastest way for a knowledgeable reader to write the account
off — and the readers who spot it are exactly the ones worth reaching.

| Grade in the ledger | How it may be phrased publicly |
|---|---|
| `supports` / `contradicts`, `[V]` or `[T]` | State it as a finding. "Four of seven said…" |
| `ambiguous` | State it as an open question. "I still can't tell whether…" |
| `secondary_research` | Attribute the source. "Per their own job postings…" |
| Untested assumption, `[H]` / `[U]` | Frame as a hypothesis being tested, never as knowledge. |

A post that says "here's what I don't know yet" is more credible than one that claims
certainty, and it draws better replies — people correct confident wrongness and ignore
confident rightness.

---

## Contradiction is content

When the evidence contradicts the founder's own earlier public position, that is a *good*
post, not an embarrassment. "I said X three weeks ago. Talked to N more people. I was
wrong, and here's the specific thing I had backwards" outperforms almost anything else
this account can post, and it is the single most credible signal available to a founder
doing public discovery.

Look for these in `hunch-lineage.md` — a retired or superseded hunch is a finished story
with a beginning and an end.
