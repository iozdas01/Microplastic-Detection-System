# Audience variants — the order, and what each one checks

The claims barely change between these. The **order** changes completely, and so does what
the listener is doing while you talk. Getting the order wrong is how a pitch that contains
every true thing still produces nothing.

## Discovery

**Who:** a fitter, a dealer, a manufacturer — anyone who might carry the problem.

**Order:** who you are (one clause) → what you are trying to understand → one question about
a specific past event. Three sentences, then stop.

**Contains no solution at all.** Not a product, not a company, not "I'm building". The moment
a solution enters, the other person stops reporting what happened and starts reacting to your
idea, politely, and everything after that is contaminated. This is the single most consequential
rule in the file.

**They are checking:** whether you understand their world well enough to be worth ten minutes.
Vocabulary and specificity do that. Ambition does not.

**Ask about the last one, not about the general case.** "How do you handle X?" gets a process
description, which is fiction. "Tell me about the last one that went wrong" gets a story, which
is data.

## Investor

**Who:** someone deciding whether to put money in, or to introduce you to people who will.

**Order:** mechanism → who is already paying for it → what you would own → why you specifically
→ beachhead and where it goes.

**They are checking the arithmetic.** This is the difference from every other audience. Any
market number you offer will be divided by something and compared to a fund return. So:

- **Never open with market size.** It invites the division before they have any reason to want
  the answer to come out well.
- **Name the denominator of every number.** Manufacturing shipments, retail sales, and a
  serviceable software ceiling are three different numbers for the same industry and they can
  differ by an order of magnitude. Quoting the wrong one is not a rounding error, it is a
  different claim.
- **If the ledger already contains the scale arithmetic, lead the ambition with it.** A founder
  who has already computed why the narrow version does not reach venture scale, and says so,
  is in a completely different conversation from one who has not been asked yet.
- **Distinguish the beachhead from the market out loud.** A small countable market is a
  strength when it is named as a place to learn, and a fatal weakness when it is presented as
  the destination.

**Volunteer, before they find it:** the numbers you have not measured, the buyer you have not
identified, and any incumbent already doing part of what you describe. All three are cheap to
say and expensive to be caught on.

**The ask is a separate decision.** Money, an introduction, or judgement on a specific open
question — decide which before the meeting. A pitch with no ask gets a good conversation and
no outcome.

## Advisor

**Who:** an operator or domain expert whose judgement you want, often someone who has already
pointed you somewhere.

**Order:** mechanism → the open question you cannot resolve → what you have tried → the
specific judgement you want from them.

**They are checking whether the mechanism is real,** and they will attack it from experience.
That is the value — an advisor who agrees with everything has told you nothing.

**Bring the disagreement forward.** If the ledger contains evidence that cuts against your own
hunch, lead with it. It converts the conversation from a performance into a working session,
and it is the fastest way to find out whether they know something you do not.

## Co-founder or early hire

**Order:** the belief → the edge → what the first year actually looks like → what is unknown.

**They are checking whether they would be useful and whether you are honest.** Overstating
traction to someone who will discover the truth in week one is the worst trade available.

Say what is not built, what is not known, and what you would need them to own.

---

## The deflection

For when a discovery conversation turns and they ask what you are building before you have the
answers. Deflect once, honestly, then hand the question back:

> Still working that out — I keep finding that {the fragile part} is where it breaks, so right
> now I'm just trying to understand how often that actually happens. What's your sense?

If they ask a second time they have earned it. Give the investor or advisor version briefly,
then stop talking and go back to their story.
