# Belief

> Custom cabinetry costs roughly three times stock cabinetry, and most of that premium is
> not manufacturing — it is a person at a desk drawing the kitchen. That design labour is
> now automatable, and whoever automates it can sell a custom kitchen at a semi-custom price.

## How the founder states it

The industry reads "custom is expensive" as a manufacturing fact. It is not. A nested-base
CNC router and an edgebander will cut, bore and band a fourteen-box kitchen in under six
hours of machine time. What actually costs money is the thirteen and a half hours a designer
spends measuring the room, drawing layouts, revising them, quoting, and turning the approved
drawing into a cut list, a G-code program and a hardware schedule. That is 52% of the human
labour in the business and it is the reason a custom kitchen is quoted at three times a
catalogue one.

It is also the reason a cabinet shop cannot grow. The machines idle at 60% while the one
designer is saturated. Buying robots for the shop floor would automate the station that
already has spare capacity. The binding constraint is a person at a desk, and that is the
station a language model plus a constraint solver can actually take over.

The industry is 6,118 US shops sharing $16.02bn of revenue, overwhelmingly small. That
fragmentation is what makes the thesis credible: nobody in it has the engineering budget to
build the thing that removes their own bottleneck.

## Belief boundaries

- In scope: fitted cabinetry and joinery — kitchens first — where the product is engineered
  per job from a room's real dimensions, and where a designer currently stands between the
  customer and the price. Frameless (European 32mm) construction from pre-finished sheet
  goods, doors and fronts bought in.
- Out of scope: freestanding furniture sold from a catalogue (measured demand for "custom"
  there runs under 1% of generic, fourteen times thinner than cabinets); anything requiring
  upholstery, foam or sewing; anything needing a finishing department — no spray booth, no
  cure room, no moulder. Also out of scope: automating the shop floor, which is the station
  with 40% spare capacity.

## Starting point and SISP check

- Starting solution, technology, or analogy: an autonomous panel factory selling configurable
  furniture direct to consumers. The cabinet framing came second, out of measurement.
- Origin: solution_or_analogy_first
- Problem stated without the solution: "When a job does not fit a dealer's catalogue sizes,
  the dealer either loses it or waits eight to twelve weeks for a local shop, because pricing
  and engineering a one-off kitchen takes a skilled person the better part of two days."
- Founder-reported observations (context, not independent evidence):
  - The original autonomous-furniture thesis was measured and the measurement moved it: across
    24 furniture and joinery categories, custom cabinets was the only one where search carried
    commercial intent, and it ran 14× the custom share of any furniture article.
  - Modelling the plant from station cycle times put the constraint in the front office, not
    on the floor — the opposite of where the founder started.
- SISP status: possible
- What the shotgun must test independently:
  - Whether dealers, designers and remodelers actually lose or delay out-of-catalogue jobs,
    described in their own words, without the vocabulary of automation, AI or factories.
  - Whether the thirteen and a half hours of design labour is real inside live shops, or an
    artefact of the founder's own build model.

## What would threaten the belief itself

- Live cabinet shops reporting that design and engineering is a small share of their labour,
  or already handled by software they are happy with (2020 Design, Cabinet Vision, Mozaik).
- Dealers reporting that their existing catalogue lines already absorb out-of-catalogue jobs,
  so the loss the belief assumes does not occur.
- Custom pricing turning out to be driven by materials, finishing or installation rather than
  by design labour — which would mean removing the designer does not move the price.
