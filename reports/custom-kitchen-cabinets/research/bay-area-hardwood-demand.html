The finding

The custom slice of the Bay Area dining-furniture market can be sized two ways from independent data. The two answers do not agree, and the disagreement is more useful than either number.

Demand-side · what people search for $3.8m /yr

Bay Area dining-furniture pool × the share of California search volume that carries custom intent × a 5× price multiple for bespoke work.

Supply-side · what makers actually pay staff $47.2m /yr

The $14.3m of annual payroll that Census County Business Patterns records for the 38 wood household furniture establishments in the 10 Bay Area counties, at a 3.3× revenue-to-payroll multiple.

Both cannot be right. The likeliest reading is that the category is real but is not bought through search. Custom furniture moves through interior designers, architects, showroom referral and repeat trade — channels that leave almost no search footprint. If that is true, the go-to-market question is not which keywords to buy. It is which twenty designers to know.

Which furniture people want customised

Given that someone wants something custom-made, what is it? This sweeps 16 categories against the same anchor — custom furniture = 1.000 — across five years in California.

Two corrections have to be applied before the raw numbers mean anything. First, several categories are inflated by accessories that share the phrase — custom sofa (16% genuine), custom bench (45% genuine), custom bar (48% genuine). Each category's index is discounted here by the share of its related search volume carrying an accessory modifier. Second, scale without buying intent is worth little, so the last column counts related queries containing near me, cost, price, companies or installation — someone shopping, not browsing.

Only 1 categories have both scale and buying intent, and neither is freestanding furniture. People want built-in storage customised — cabinets and closets. Custom wardrobe alone outranks the entire custom furniture category and custom dining tables run at 380% of it with not a single buying-intent query attached.

The evidence behind those two is unambiguous. For custom wardrobe the top related queries are custom closet (100), ikea custom wardrobe (79), ikea wardrobe (78), ikea (78).

By contrast custom dining table returns exactly one related query — the plural of itself. There is not enough surrounding search behaviour to form a cluster, which is consistent with the divergence above: the demand exists, but it is not expressed as search.

What this changes. The same shop, the same hardwood, the same CNC and finishing line can make a closet system or a run of cabinetry as readily as a dining table — but the cabinet buyer is searching, price-checking and location-filtering right now, and the table buyer is not. If the plan depends on being findable, the wedge is built-in storage. If the plan depends on designer relationships, the table is fine and search volume was never going to measure it either way.

What the data says

Tier 2 · CEX

The pool is $138.9m a year, not a slice of a billion-dollar abstraction

3,157,577 Bay Area households × the measured Consumer Expenditure Survey spend on kitchen and dining room furniture. Cross-checked against actual retail sales: the build puts US kitchen-and-dining spend at 4.0% of all NAICS 442 store sales, which is the right shape for one room out of a category that covers the whole house.

Tier 2 · CEX

Even the richest fifth of households spend $92 a year on dining furniture

That is the ceiling the whole model sits under. A $4,200 table is not an annual purchase out of a furniture budget — it is a once-a-decade or once-a-lifetime purchase. Acquisition maths has to survive that, because there is no second sale to amortise it against.

Tier 1 · Trends

Custom framing is 0.55% of the search volume of the generic term

Across five years in California, custom dining table runs at well under one percent of dining table. The rising queries are dominated by seating capacity, extendability and named retail brands — not by bespoke framing. That is a product signal as much as a marketing one.

Tier 2 · PPI

Input costs have run 14 points ahead of output prices since 2020

Hardwood lumber is up 42.7% since January 2020; the producer price index for nonupholstered wood household furniture is up 28.7%. Domestic makers absorbed the difference, and employment in furniture manufacturing fell 13.7% over the same period. Anyone entering is entering a margin squeeze already in progress.

Tier 2 · PEP

The Bay Area has lost 519,506 people to domestic migration since 2020

Offset only recently by international arrivals. Moving is the single largest trigger of a furniture purchase, so net-negative domestic migration is a demand headwind that national furniture numbers hide completely.

Why the Census key is first, and what it would move

The supply-side figure — the one that contradicts the search-based sizing — is built from one measured number and one assumed one. The measured half is the annual payroll County Business Patterns records for the Bay Area establishments. The assumed half is the multiple converting payroll to revenue.

The Economic Census publishes total receipts for NAICS 337122 directly. With a Census key the multiple stops being an assumption and becomes a measurement, and the central claim on this page either firms up or falls over. That is a two-minute signup standing between the analysis and its own load-bearing number.

How this was built

Fifteen collectors, ten of which need no credentials at all, landing 21,573 rows. Each writes the verbatim API payload to data/raw/, a tidy table to data/processed/, and one line to data/MANIFEST.jsonl recording the URL, fetch time, row count and status. Nothing downstream quotes a number without a manifest line behind it.

Two substitutions were needed. USITC DataWeb requires an account token, so import figures come from UN Comtrade's public endpoint — the same underlying US statistics, same HS lines. The Census MRTS API is now key-gated, so retail sales come from the published workbook, which carries the identical series back to 1992.

This page is generated by scripts/build_page.py from the collector outputs — every figure, table and derived claim above is computed, not typed. The model's assumptions all live in one block at the top of scripts/build_tam.py. Change an assumption there, re-run the pipeline, and this page rewrites itself.
