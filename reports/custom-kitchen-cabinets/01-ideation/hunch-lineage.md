### Statement

We believe **kitchen and bath dealers, interior designers and design-build remodelers**
lose or delay jobs that fall outside their catalogue sizes because **the only alternative is
a local custom shop where a designer spends roughly thirteen hours measuring, drawing,
revising and engineering the job before a firm price exists**, and **language models plus
constraint solvers can now take a room's real dimensions to an engineered, machine-ready,
priced cabinet run** — which makes a supplier who returns a firm custom price in ten minutes
viable now.

### Components

- Segment: kitchen & bath dealers, interior designers and design-build remodelers in one US
  metro — the trade channel that specifies fitted cabinetry, not the homeowner.
- Problem: out-of-catalogue jobs are lost, discounted, or pushed onto an eight-to-twelve-week
  local shop, because pricing and engineering a one-off run is two days of skilled labour.
- Mechanism: 52% of the human labour in a custom cabinet business is design and engineering,
  not making. Remove it and the same machines sell a custom kitchen at a semi-custom price.
- Why now: a constraint solver over clearances, code and standard box widths, plus a language
  model that turns a customer's words and a homeowner-taken laser measurement into a storage
  allocation, is a 2024-2026 capability. Neither half worked reliably before.
- Existing workaround: the dealer quotes the catalogue line and takes the filler-strip
  compromise, sends the job to a local shop, or walks away from it.
- Plausible buyer: the dealer or remodeler, who resells at their own margin — not the
  homeowner. The homeowner-direct configurator is a later door, not this one.
- Cheapest next test: build a list of ~70 trade targets in one metro and call ten of them
  with one question — "when a job doesn't fit your catalogue sizes, what do you do?" — and
  write the answers down verbatim without pitching.
- Hunch disconfirmation: if the majority of ten dealers say their existing line handles
  out-of-catalogue work, or that the loss is rare and cheap, the wedge is not there.

### Evidence

- Supports: E1, E2, E3, E6 — all desk measurement. No buyer has been asked anything yet.
- Contradicts: none logged.
- Still unknown: everything on the demand side. The design-labour split (E4, E5) is derived
  from the founder's own build model, not observed in a live shop.

### Relationship to belief

- How this hunch expresses the belief: it names one channel (the trade), one product (fitted
  frameless kitchens) and one observable loss (out-of-catalogue jobs) for the belief's general
  claim that design labour, not manufacturing, is what makes custom expensive.
- Evidence that would threaten only this hunch: dealers confirming the design bottleneck but
  refusing to buy through a supplier they cannot visit, or insisting on a showroom display
  before specifying. That moves the channel, not the belief.
- Evidence that would threaten the underlying belief: shops reporting design is a small share
  of their cost, or that existing cabinet software already removes it.
