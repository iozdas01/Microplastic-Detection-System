Microfibre Market Ladder

The left column is what the sensor can do. The right column is who can be sold to once it can do that, and for how much. Each rung opens only when the rung below it is built, so the market does not grow by selling harder — it grows when the measurement changes.

you are here V1

Inline polymer identification

Particle type and particle count, in the pipe, in minutes. One measurement point per site.

Replaces work that is done today by sending a sample to a lab for three to four weeks, or by a specialist running FTIR microscopy for a full day per sample.

V1

Fashion brands with a microfibre programme

$2.5M / year

50 brands paying $50,000 a year for continuous, site-level polymer identification across their wet-processing suppliers. The brand count is the top of a researched 20–50 range; the price is the midpoint of a $25k–100k band.

They pay because the industry's own test method is gravimetric and first-wash only: it weighs fibre and identifies nothing. No existing product gives them type at site level.

50 brands × $50k

V1 · same hardware

Benchmarking rig for filtration builders

The same instrument, used to prove what a filter captures and what slips through, by polymer rather than by mass.

2ᵃ

Filtration and separation companies

not sized · nobody has counted them

The only buyers who have asked for this measurement unprompted, because none of them can prove what their equipment removes without sending samples to a lab. We know eight by name and no source publishes a count, so this row stays blank until it is counted rather than filled with a number we made up.

Two categories, and they are not the same deal. Sector specialists like Matter sit deep inside one industry — microfibre filters in textile mills and washing machines — so what they carry is access to the mills and brands we already target. Horizontal separation companies like Cyfract sell one physical principle into many industries at once, so what they carry is entry to water we would otherwise never see.

count × price · both still unknown

V1 · partner-led

Same instrument, unfamiliar water

A horizontal separation partner drags the sensor into water it was never designed for. Each vertical needs its own calibration, and some need optical changes: paper white water, for one, sits past where turbidity instruments saturate.

The partner pays for the proof they need. We keep the calibration.

2ᵇ

Industries we could not open ourselves

not sized · next to research

Cyfract names wastewater treatment, desalination, mining, oil and gas, and agriculture as its markets. Paper reaches us the same way: as a pilot a partner is running, not as an industry we sell into.

Each one has to pass the same three tests paper failed: is anyone required or paid to know, is what we detect what they care about, and does the water break the optics.

partner count × verticals each × proof per vertical

V2

Attribution

Several points per site, so a polymer maps to a step, a machine or a fabric rather than to the factory as a whole.

Risk: if streams merge before the drain, attribution needs machine-level sampling and the hardware count per site multiplies.

V2

The same brands, far wider coverage

$14.0M / year

Coverage inside an account goes from a sample of sites to the whole supplier base. Large brands publish 104 to 269 wet-process suppliers each.

50 brands × 140 sites × $2k per site

V3

Multi-analyte on the same optical path

Residual peroxide, unfixed dye, salt and turbidity alongside fibre. These are things a mill already checks by hand with strips, titrations and grab samples.

V3

Mills become buyers in their own right

$120M hardware

The mill stops buying a compliance instrument and starts buying process instrumentation out of an existing quality budget. Plus $48M a year in service.

16,000 sites × 3 units × $2.5k

V4

Closed loop on one decision

Rinse endpoint first: stop rinsing when the water is actually clean, rather than by eye. Hot water and fuel come straight off the bill.

V4

Mills, paying out of what they save

$1.17B / year

Efficiency programmes deliver $230,000 to $730,000 a year per mill for $110,000 to $300,000 of capital, at three to ten month paybacks. Taking a tenth of the top of that range across every documented site is the ceiling, not a forecast.

16,000 sites × 10% × $730k

V5

The data network

Verified, continuous, site-level polymer data across thousands of mills. Nothing new in the box; the value is the accumulated record.

V5

Brands, and regulators when rules land

$30M / year

About 300 brand-side organisations paying for supplier data they currently take on trust. The smallest layer on this ladder, not the largest.

300 organisations × $100k

Paper mills, tested and ruled out as a direct market: no rule requires particle or microplastic monitoring there and the EU wastewater directive excludes them; mills' own treatment already removes over 99% of microplastics; their real pain is stickies, defined by tackiness and therefore invisible to optical contrast, with no reliable measurement method by the industry's own account; inline optical particle counting is already sold there by an incumbent; and white water sits past where turbidity instruments saturate, so it needs an optical redesign rather than a recalibration. It reaches us only one way — as a place a filtration partner is piloting and needs proof, which is a sale to that partner, not to the paper industry.
