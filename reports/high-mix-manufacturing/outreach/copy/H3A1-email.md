# H3 — email copy

## C93 · Susan Lind Chastain Inc · Christopher Adams (VP)

status: **SENT 2026-09-03 by the founder, by hand** · channel: email · stage: msg1
Follows a phone call the same day in which they asked the founder to email.

**Subject:** Following up on our call, and a visit to the workroom

> Hi Christopher,
>
> Thanks for taking my call today.
>
> I have attached two photos: one of a window with the single measurement I managed to take, and one of the blinds as they are now. I tried to measure these myself and I do not trust the numbers. The windows are awkward to reach and I could not get a reading I would stand behind, which is honestly what prompted the call in the first place.
>
> I understand you work through interior designers rather than directly with homeowners, so do tell me if I am in the wrong place for the job itself.
>
> The bigger reason I am writing is this. I have a manufacturing engineering background and I am looking closely at how a measurement travels from someone's window to the workroom that actually makes the piece. Yours is the part of that chain I understand least. Would you be open to me coming by Natoma Street and watching how it really works? Half an hour, no sales agenda. I would rather see it than ask about it.
>
> What does your week look like?
>
> Best,
> Izgin

Attachments: two founder photos, one window with a single measurement, one of the existing blinds.

| Choice | Reason |
|---|---|
| Leads with the founder's own failed measurement | It is true. The founder has windows, tried to measure them, and does not trust the numbers. A real job is a stronger opening than a research request, and it is not a pretext. |
| Names the designer channel outright | They are a workroom selling through interior designers rather than to homeowners (public listings, verified 2026-09-03). Asking directly avoids a "come through your designer" reply that would waste the best premises contact in the batch. |
| States the research reason plainly | Presenting purely as a customer and being found out later would cost the relationship, and this is the contact most likely to allow a site visit. |
| No scheduling link | LR-M9. "What does your week look like" keeps the work on the sender. |
| No "would software help" | That exact question already produced the batch's one false positive (E11, Stoneside). It is not asked here and must not be asked on the visit. |

**Why this contact matters.** They are a workroom, so their chain carries a handoff the
retailers do not have: a designer measures on site, and the workroom fabricates from that
number. That is a second place the dimension can be corrupted, and it is the least understood
part of H3A2.

**Questions for the visit, if it happens.** All past-tense, none hypothetical:

- "When a designer sends you measurements, how often do you send them back?"
- "What does a designer's measurement actually arrive as: a form, an email, a photo of a sketch?"
- "Tell me about the last piece you had to remake. Where did the number go wrong?"
- "Do you ever go and measure yourself instead of trusting what came in?" A yes means the
  workroom is absorbing the cost, which is a direct H3A3 answer.

**Open risk.** If the founder's blinds are hard venetians or rollers, this is a soft
furnishings workroom and the job itself is not theirs. That does not affect the visit.
