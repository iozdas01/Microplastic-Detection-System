# Cambridge reconnect — recovering the Universal Wolf contact

## The situation

The founder ran a CAD-CAM interoperability case study with **Universal Wolf** during the
Cambridge ISMM MPhil (2024-10 to 2025-08). The main contact there ran the CAD side and, by the
end of the study, was describing a version of the tool he would have bought. His name is in a
Cambridge mailbox the founder can no longer easily reach.

**Universal Wolf** is a complex sheet metal fabricator in Blyth, Northumberland — formerly
Tharsus Engineering, rebranded. Design for manufacture, NPI, CNC profiling, laser, brake press,
welding, paint and assembly. High mix, per-order engineering: exactly the founder's declared
belief scope.

## The one change I made to the ask

The founder's instinct was to ask Sam for the **name**. The draft asks for the **introduction**
instead, and that is deliberate.

That contact met the founder as a participant in a university research study, not as a sales
lead. Sam owns that relationship. Asking him for a name and then cold-approaching the man
commercially spends Sam's goodwill without asking, and it is the version most likely to end
with an awkward reply and no introduction. Asking Sam to make the introduction costs him about
the same effort, gives him the choice, and arrives at the contact warm instead of cold.

**Do not mention the LOI in this email.** It is the right instinct and the wrong message to put
it in. Telling a former supervisor you want a letter of intent from his research participant
turns a favour into a commercial request he has to think about. Get the introduction, have the
conversation, and raise commercial terms with the contact directly once he has said the problem
is still live.

# On the LOI

Worth doing, and not yet. The sequence that works: introduction, then a call establishing the
problem is still live and still costs him something, then terms. An LOI asked for before that
conversation is a document about a product nobody has re-confirmed they want, and it is
exactly the kind of soft artefact that reads as traction without being it.

When it is time, note that he is one company and a warm one. An LOI from a case-study
participant who already likes you is real, but it evidences that *he* would buy — not that a
market would. Log it as one contact's commitment, not as validation of H2.
