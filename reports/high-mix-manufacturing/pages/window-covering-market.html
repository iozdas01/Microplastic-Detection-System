What that shape means — and what it hides

Among specialist window-treatment stores, the trade is tiny and fragmented: of 2,170 establishments, 1,592 have fewer than five people and only 41 have twenty or more.

But that code is not the market. Census classifies an establishment by its primary activity, so the largest sellers of made-to-measure window coverings in America are counted somewhere else entirely and are invisible above:

verified Home Depot and Lowe's both sell custom window coverings with measure-and-install services, and are classified as home centres, not window-treatment stores.

Home Depot has owned Blinds.com since 2014. Both subcontract the measurement itself to third-party local installers rather than employing measurers.

the spread worth explaining Lowe's charges $45 for a professional measure. The national retailer in E8 charges $225.

Five times, for nominally the same visit. Either big box is subsidising it as a cost of winning the sale, or E8 is pricing it at something closer to true cost. Which one is right decides who actually feels this — and it is a better question than the remake rate.

not countable here E-commerce (SelectBlinds, Blinds Galore), franchise networks (Budget Blinds, 3 Day Blinds) and big-box programmes.

These sit under electronic shopping, under building-finishing contractors, or inside a home centre's line items. No single NAICS code contains the made-to-measure window covering trade, so no top-down count of it is available at all — which is the strongest argument on this page for sizing bottom-up from calls.

Scale exists in this industry. It is just not where a specialist retail code looks. It sits in 329 manufacturers, whose largest two dozen hold most of the employment; in 7,960 installation contractors employing 78,981 people, who are who big box pushes the measuring onto; and in a handful of national retail programmes that no code isolates.

The one number that unlocks the model

Jobs per dealer per year

You have the price of a measure visit and the number of dealers. This is the only missing multiplier between them and a defensible bottom-up figure. One question, to one dealer.

Four ways in

Ordered by what the evidence currently supports, not by ambition.

ASell the measurement to vertically integrated dealers
Who pays
Operators who run their own factories and their own measuring crews — the Stoneside shape.
Why it fits
They carry both costs, so one sale removes a cost and a constraint. Few dozen targets, not thousands.
Evidence for
E8 gives a priced visit and a radius limit. E9 says every dealer sends a human and none uses software.
What kills it
If the visit is a sales call as much as a measurement, removing it removes the sale.
BSell remake reduction to manufacturers
Who pays
The 329 manufacturers, concentrated enough that two dozen matter.
Why it fits
Real firms with cost lines and quality functions. A remake hits cost of goods and they track it monthly.
Evidence for
Sun Glow publishes the arithmetic to its own dealers. Nothing first-hand yet.
What kills it
H3A2. If the error is the dealer's number and not the factory's cut, the manufacturer cannot fix it and will not buy.
CSell software to the long tail of dealers
Who pays
1,592 specialist shops with under five people — plus an uncounted long tail of installers and franchisees.
Why it is listed third
Largest count, and the evidence is against it. The owner does the visit himself, so his time is not a line item to remove.
Evidence against
E10 — family-run owners were unenthusiastic, unprompted. E11 is the only positive and it is a Mom Test false positive, logged at 2/5.
What would revive it
A reason from E10's owners. A no with a reason is worth more than the yes in E11.
DSell into a big-box measure-and-install programme
Who pays
Home Depot, Lowe's, or the third-party networks that run their measure services.
Why it fits
The only genuine enterprise buyer in the chain. They run measurement at national scale, subcontract it, and carry the remake liability on their own guarantee.
Evidence for
None first-hand. Everything here is public record, and no one at either has been contacted.
What kills it
Procurement cycles measured in years, and the $45 price point suggests they already treat measurement as a subsidised loss-leader rather than a cost to optimise.
EBecome the dealer
Who pays
Consumers, directly.
Why it fits
Own both ends, which is what the belief argues for, and there is existing CNC capacity to point at it.
What kills it
Customer acquisition, every time. The record already in this repo says owning a factory does not fix CAC — Model No. owned its factory and its configurator and was absorbed anyway.
Test before building
Not a software test. Sell one job.

What to do next

Option A remains where a priced pain, a named constraint and a reachable buyer line up today, and its next question is free: how many jobs a month do you turn down because the drive is too far, and how far is too far? That converts a complaint into a lost-revenue number without mentioning a product.

Option D is now on the board because the enterprise buyer turned out to exist — it was hidden by a classification code rather than absent from the market. Before spending a week on it, settle the cheap question the $45-versus-$225 spread raises: is the measure visit a cost they want removed, or a sales call they are paying to keep? Ask an installer who works a big-box programme; they will know, and they are reachable.
