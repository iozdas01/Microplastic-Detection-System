Seven layers, seven numbers, seven calls

The same failure — a wrong measurement — costs something different at every layer of this trade. So you do not need one number for "the market". You need seven small ones, and each is obtainable from one conversation with someone reachable.

Census counts generated from window_covering_structure.csv. Taxonomy and sizing formulas authored in scripts/research/build_players_page.py — do not edit this file.

Why layer by layer

A top-down number does not exist here: no single NAICS code contains this trade, and the biggest sellers are filed under home centres. That is usually bad news. Here it is not, because every layer's cost shows up on a different line and each one is separately askable. Size them one at a time and they cross-check each other.

The layers

LAYER 1Independent workroom that fabricates

Makes the product, measures it, fits it. One P&L carries the whole chain.

Feels
Everything. Material and labour twice on a remake, plus the measuring visit itself.
Sizing formula
jobs/yr × $225 ÷ annual revenue = share of revenue spent measuring
Already have
$225 per visit — E8, n=1
Still need
jobs per year · annual revenue
Who to call
The owner. Answers the phone himself.
Reachability
easy
This is the layer the founder has already reached. Two more questions finish it.

Buys product from Hunter Douglas, Springs or similar. Measures, sells, installs. Does not fabricate.

Feels
The measuring visit, and the reorder cost when the wrong number was theirs.

jobs/yr × visit cost + remakes/yr × reorder cost
Already have
nothing first-hand
Still need
jobs per year · who is billed when the number was wrong
Who to call
Dealer principal.
Reachability
easy
The bulk of the specialist retail count. Same call shape as layer 1.

LAYER 4Vertically integrated national

3 Day Blinds (owned by Hunter Douglas), Stoneside. Own factories, own measurers, own installers.

Feels
All of it, on one P&L, and they can attribute it properly because they own every step.
Sizing formula
remake cost + measure cost, both as a share of revenue
Already have
Stoneside: free measure visit, customer liable if they self-measure, 4–5 week lead time — E7, E9
Still need
remake rate · what the measure operation costs as a share of revenue
Who to call
Operations director. Stoneside is already a warm contact.
Reachability
medium
Best single P&L to understand, because nobody else can separate the costs cleanly.
LAYER 5Big box1 call ≈ 100 businesses

Home Depot (owns Blinds.com since 2014), Lowe's, Costco. Sell manufacturer product; subcontract measure and install to third-party local installers.

Feels
A subcontractor invoice and a guarantee liability. They have pushed the labour off their own books.
Sizing formula
measure jobs/yr × subcontract rate + guarantee claims × remake cost
Already have
Lowe's charges $45 for a professional measure — public
Still need
is $45 the true cost or a subsidised loss-leader · remake rate on the programme
Who to call
Not corporate. An installer who works the programme. They know the rate and the economics and they are reachable.
Reachability
installer easy · corporate very hard
The $45-versus-$225 spread is the sharpest open question in this market. Five times, for nominally the same visit.
LAYER 6Online, customer self-measures1 call ≈ 100 businesses

Blinds.com, SelectBlinds, Blinds Galore, Wayfair. No visit at all — the buyer measures their own window.

Feels
Pure remake liability through the fit guarantees. SelectBlinds' FIT Protection and Blinds.com's SureFit both pay for the CUSTOMER'S error.
Sizing formula
orders/yr × guarantee claim rate × remake cost
Already have
the guarantees exist and are given away at checkout — public record
Still need
what share of orders are remade under the guarantee
Who to call
Operations or customer service manager.
Reachability
hard
The cleanest number in the entire market. No professional visit confounds it, so their remake rate is a direct measurement of how often a human mismeasures a window. Every other layer's rate is contaminated by the visit.
LAYER 7Manufacturers

Hunter Douglas (largest worldwide), Springs Window Fashions, Norman, plus the long tail. Cut to a dimension somebody else supplied.

Feels
Product and labour twice when the fault is theirs; they bill the dealer when it is not.
Sizing formula
orders/yr × remake rate × (material + labour)
Already have
Sun Glow publishes the dealer arithmetic: one remake wipes 1.0–2.3 comparable jobs by margin
Still need
remake rate as a share of orders · the split between own-fault and dealer-fault
Who to call
Production or quality manager. They track it monthly; it hits cost of goods.
Reachability
medium
H3A1 as written targets this layer. The calls so far have all been the retail side.

Worked example — layer 1, the founder's own

The obvious model has two unknowns — jobs per year and annual revenue — but they cancel. Measuring as a share of revenue is just $225 divided by what an average job is worth. One unknown, and it is the easier one to ask for:

Average job valueShare of revenue spent measuringWorth removing?
$750 job30.0%yes
$1,000 job22.5%yes
$1,500 job15.0%yes
$2,500 job9.0%yes
$4,000 job5.6%marginal
$6,000 job3.8%marginal

This is the number that decides whether layer 1 is a business or a rounding error, and it swings by eight times across a plausible range of job values. On a small single-room job the visit eats a fifth of the revenue; on a whole-house job it is noise. So the question is not "how big is the market" but "how small are the jobs" — which is also, neatly, why big box can charge $45 and E8 charges $225.

Jobs per year is still needed, but only for the absolute figure:

800 jobs$180,000$15,000

$225 per visit is measured (E8, n=1). Both remaining unknowns — average job value and jobs per year — come from one conversation with any independent, and the founder has already had four of these calls without asking either.

Which calls to make first

Not in layer order. In leverage order.

Two more independents1finishes the worked exampleeasy

The franchise call is the outlier and it is worth saying plainly: a franchisor already aggregates remake data across a four-figure number of businesses, because remakes flow through the product orders they negotiate on the network's behalf. That is a published-nowhere number that one person can hand you.

The one number that is cleanest

Layer 6. An online retailer's remake rate is the only figure in this market uncontaminated by a professional visit — the customer measured, so a remake means a human mismeasured a window, full stop. Every other layer's rate mixes customer error, surveyor error, factory tolerance and out-of-square openings. If one number is worth being persistent for, it is that one.
