The human is the integration layer

EF Form SCI · week 6
due 2026-09-06

Making one of something should cost what making a thousand costs per unit. We are building the app that configures the machines so it can — starting with made-to-measure window coverings.

Problem

Interoperability is a fixed cost, paid only when there is a production run to spread it over. Made-to-order has no run — so a person retypes the number between design, machine and order system instead. That person is the integration layer, and in window coverings they cost $225 a visit.

  • That retailer told us the $225 visit is still "not accurate enough" — and they decline jobs when the drive is too far, so it caps their radius. E8
  • Inside the largest player, a measure technician put wrong-size orders at roughly 50/50 between his own error and the factory's. Half the errors survive a trained person. E15
  • The industry already prices this. SelectBlinds sells a free-remake guarantee at checkout; Sun Glow tells dealers one remake wipes out 1.5 similar jobs. public source
  • And the demand is already there: 31.9% of the US retail window-treatment line was bought from sellers with no shop, against 8.8% e-commerce across all retail. E18

Solution

Collapse the fixed cost — the app configures the machines, so it is paid per job rather than per production run. Anthropic previewed the Model Hardware Standard on 27 August: machine integration from weeks to hours.

  • Own the chain — a customisable furniture factory on CNC capacity we already run. Manufacturing revenue, not a seat fee. about us
  • Or route it — the app drives whichever machines the shop already has.
  • One test decides: whether a measurement taken without a trained person can be trusted. If yes, routing works and the visit disappears. If no, you inherit the $225 and only owning the chain pays. E8

  • A year at Cambridge on machine-readable annotation of engineering drawings — this exact handoff, measured. about us
  • IMTEK CNC capacity in Türkiye plus EU customs union freight. about us

Market

  • The beachhead is countable: a $9.63bn US retail window-treatment line made by 320 manufacturers, ten of whom hold 62.5% of revenue. E19 E12
  • It is not the market. Everything cut to a site-taken dimension is a $108.5bn US pool — carpentry, cabinets, windows and doors, glazing, stone. Blinds is 4.6% of it. E13
  • We take manufacturing revenue rather than a software licence, so the ceiling is the category itself and not what two thousand small firms can afford in seat fees. E13

What we do not know yet

  • The misfit rate. Nobody publishes it, we have not measured it, and the thesis moves on it. Ten conversations running now. E15
  • Why we are not the graveyard. Model No. owned its factory and a configurator; Interior Define went asset-light. Both died of customer acquisition cost, which neither owning a factory nor better software fixes. public source
  • Who pays. Manufacturer, dealer and consumer each eat it in different cases, and family-run owners were cool on software — pointing at ~10 enterprise buyers, not PLG. E10 E12
  • If integration is being standardised and open-sourced, the moat is not connecting machines. Our answer is the judgement above the interface — and an installed base that is not MHS-ready: one machine on a blind fabricator's floor is. E20
