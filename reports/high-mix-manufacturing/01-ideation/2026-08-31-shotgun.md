## Belief

> The problem is that software and machines don't talk, and you have to own both to fix it.

Scope under test: manufacturing where the design-to-machine handoff is **per-order and
currently manual**. Industry deliberately unfixed — selecting it was this run's job.

## Intake Starting Point and SISP Test

Starting solution: "vibe manufacturing" — a D2C website where a customer specifies what they
need and a factory the company owns makes it. Furniture as entry wedge advised by angel
Daniel Theobald on the grounds that wood is easier to work than metal. Intake status:
**`probable`**.

**The required independent test was NOT run.** `belief.md` requires the pain to be shown in
operators' own language, without solution vocabulary, before any solution-shaped research.
The community collector was rate-limited to zero records (`source-status.json`), so this run
carries **no operator voice at all**. Everything below is competitive and historical desk
evidence.

That matters most for the demand-side claims, which remain exactly as unproven as they were
this morning. It matters least for the competitive findings, which are strong.

## Contradictions and Counterevidence

**1. The counter-query inverted the graveyard reading.** Asking who *succeeded* rather than
who died:

| | Xometry (asset-light) | Protolabs (asset-heavy) |
|---|---|---|
| Quarterly revenue | **$229M, +41% YoY** | $149.3M, +10.6% |
| Gross margin | 35.7% | **44%** |
| Factories | **none** | ~1M sq ft owned |

The company that owns no machines is bigger and growing ~4x faster. The one that owns them
earns more per dollar. **Both are alive.** Direct evidence against "you *have to* own both
ends" as a necessity claim — and it partially satisfies disconfirmer #1, written before this
was known.

**2. Zero wrong-time predecessors.** Not one failure in the graveyard was caused by technology
being unready. Katerra, Lustron and the modular sector all died of operating leverage — fixed
factory cost against lumpy, dispersed, project-shaped demand. The card is explicit: wrong-idea
predecessors are counterevidence, not timing proof.

**3. Standard cabinet work is solved and users are happy.** dconti, 2.5 years on Mozaik:
*"It is a cabinet to cnc program it does that very well."* Ryan owns two top-tier Cabinet
Vision seats and *"can't remember the last time it was even opened... but Mozaik is used all
day everyday."* Link 1 fails outright here.

**4. The quoting-time spread is 25-to-80x.** Joe: *"5-15 minutes for a normal kitchen."*
Eddie: *"about an hour."* Another owner: 6-7 hours. The prior belief's 13.5-hour design-labour
figure is supported by nobody. Do not average this — it is the most interesting unexplained
variance in the run.

**5. Fab/Hem should not be cited against vertical integration.** Its integration move was a
late rescue attempt on an already-broken acquisition model, not a thesis executed from the
start. Recorded as `unknown`, not as evidence.

## Belief Status

**under tension — founder decision required.**

- **Link 1 ("the coupling is broken")** — false for standard cabinet work ($125/mo, happy
  users), false for metal parts (since 1999), false for countertops. **True in one pocket:**
  custom, non-standard millwork.
- **Link 2 ("you have to own both ends")** — not necessary. Xometry disproves necessity.
  Still *advantageous* at batch size one, where Protolabs earns 44% against 35.7%. The claim
  survives as "ownership buys unit economics at low volume", not as "you have to."
- **Why-now** — dead as written. Salvageable only in the narrowed form.

The belief is not dead. It is **much smaller than it was this morning**, and the part that
survived is not the part the founder was aiming at.
